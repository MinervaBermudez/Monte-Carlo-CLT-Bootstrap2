# Statistical Simulation in R: Monte Carlo, CLT & Bootstrap

A **reproducible statistical computing project** bringing together three of my
own university laboratory exercises. It explores Monte Carlo estimation,
sampling distributions and the central limit theorem, and nonparametric
bootstrap inference in R.

## Explore the project

| Topic | Experiments |
| --- | --- |
| Monte Carlo | Estimate π and two integrals; simulate compound Poisson–lognormal losses and compare tail quantiles. |
| Central limit theorem | Generate Weibull random variables; compare sampling means from uniform, exponential and lognormal populations; examine mean vs. median under contamination. |
| Bootstrap | Estimate the ozone median and 99th percentile; compare classical and bootstrap confidence intervals for ozone and wind means. |

## Key statistical lessons

- A Monte Carlo estimate needs an uncertainty measure; convergence is not necessarily monotonic.
- The CLT concerns **sample means**, not the shape of the raw population, and skewness can slow normal approximation.
- Bootstrap intervals depend on their resampling assumptions. An extreme quantile or temporally correlated observations deserve particular care.
